About the Knowledge Management System

Understanding how ENEC captures, shares, and preserves nuclear knowledge to support Ethiopia’s peaceful nuclear programme.

Purpose & Vision

The Ethiopian Nuclear Energy Commission (ENEC) Knowledge Management System (KMS) is designed to systematically identify, capture, organize, share, and preserve nuclear knowledge. This supports the safe, secure, and peaceful development of nuclear science and technology in Ethiopia.

Our vision is to build a sustainable knowledge culture where critical expertise is retained, easily accessible, and continuously improved — in line with international best practices promoted by the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA).

Knowledge Management Policy

ENEC is committed to treating knowledge as a strategic organizational asset. Our Knowledge Management Policy establishes the principles, responsibilities, and processes for managing nuclear knowledge across the Commission.

  • Knowledge is critical to nuclear safety, security, and programme success.
  • Both explicit and tacit knowledge must be captured and shared.
  • Access to knowledge is controlled according to classification and need-to-know.
  • All staff have a role in contributing to and using organizational knowledge.
  • Knowledge management is integrated into daily work processes.

Governance Structure

The Knowledge Management System is governed through clear roles and responsibilities:

Leadership

Senior management provides strategic direction and ensures resources for knowledge management activities.

Knowledge Management Team

Responsible for operating the KMS, maintaining standards, supporting users, and driving continuous improvement.

Knowledge Stewards

Subject-matter experts who review, validate, and maintain knowledge in their areas of responsibility.

All Staff

Every staff member is expected to contribute knowledge, share lessons learned, and use available resources.

How Knowledge is Classified

All knowledge assets in the system are classified using two main dimensions:

1. Knowledge Criticality

  • Critical — Essential for safety, security, or core programme functions. Loss would have significant impact.
  • Significant — Important for effective operations and decision-making.
  • Useful — Supportive knowledge that improves efficiency or understanding.

2. Access Level

  • Public — Freely available to everyone
  • Restricted — Available to authenticated ENEC staff and approved partners
  • Confidential — Limited to specific roles with a clear need-to-know

Alignment with IAEA Guidance

The ENEC Knowledge Management System is developed in accordance with international good practices, particularly the IAEA publication Guide to Knowledge Management Strategies and Approaches in Nuclear Energy Organizations and Facilities (NG-G-6.1) and related nuclear knowledge management guidance.

Key principles followed include:

  • Treating knowledge as a strategic asset
  • Preventing loss of critical knowledge
  • Integrating knowledge management into the management system
  • Supporting competence development and knowledge transfer
  • Promoting a knowledge-sharing culture
